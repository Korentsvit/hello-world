Programme financing

Exploring broader access to research financing

We are developing a route through which eligible investors could help finance the NWPT-SM32300 programme through a dedicated programme vehicle, potentially using permissioned tokens. This page explains our thinking and the safeguards we are building in. It is not an offer.

Important: this is not an offer or invitation to invest

Nothing on this page, or anywhere on this website, is an offer to sell, a solicitation of an offer to buy, or a recommendation regarding any security, token, digital asset or other financial product, in any jurisdiction. No such offer is currently being made. Any future offer would be made only through formal offering documents, only to persons eligible to receive them, and only where lawful.

The financing structure described here is at an early stage, has not been approved by any regulator and may change significantly or not proceed at all. [CONFIRM: final wording with legal counsel.]

Why we are describing this publicly

Drug development is expensive and usually funded by a small number of specialist investors. We are interested in whether rigorous research can also be supported by a broader group of eligible participants, including people in the crypto and mental-health communities who care about better treatments, while keeping the same standards of scientific and clinical independence.

We think people deserve to understand how a research programme is funded, and how funding is kept separate from clinical decisions. That is why we describe the approach here, before any financing is available.

What we are exploring

  • A dedicated programme vehicle. A separate legal entity that would hold the economic interest in the programme, so that participation relates specifically to NWPT-SM32300 rather than to a wider business. [PLACEHOLDER: entity type and jurisdiction, once decided.]
  • Permissioned tokens, potentially. Interests in the vehicle might be recorded as digital tokens on a blockchain. "Permissioned" means they could be held and transferred only by holders who have passed identity, eligibility and anti-money-laundering checks, and only in ways that comply with applicable law.
  • Eligibility limits. Participation would be limited to investors who are legally eligible in their jurisdiction. This is likely to mean professional, qualified or accredited investors, and to exclude some countries entirely.

Plain-language terms

Programme vehicle: a company or similar entity set up to finance one specific programme.

Token: a digital record of ownership or rights, held on a blockchain.

Permissioned: only verified, eligible holders can hold or receive the token; it cannot be freely traded.

KYC/AML: "know your customer" and anti-money-laundering checks required by law.

Clinical decisions stay independent

Financing must never influence the science or put participants at risk. These are the principles we propose to build into the structure. They are subject to approval by our board and legal advisers. [CONFIRM]

No say over the science

Investors and token holders would have no rights over study design, participant eligibility, dosing, safety decisions, data analysis or publication.

No access to participant data

Participant data would be protected under data protection law and research ethics requirements, and never shared with investors.

All results reported

Study results would be reported whatever they show, on the same timetable regardless of any effect on investors.

No investment from participants

We would not seek investment from study participants or their families, and would not use recruitment or clinical channels to promote financing.

Separate communications

Scientific updates on this website would be written for the public and patients, not to promote any financial product.

Conflicts declared

Financial interests of the leadership, clinical investigators and advisers would be declared and managed.

Risks anyone considering this should understand

If an opportunity is offered in future, it will be high risk. Formal offering documents would set out the risks in full. In summary:

  • The treatment may not work. NWPT-SM32300's effectiveness is unproven. Most investigational medicines do not reach approval, and a Phase 2B study may show no benefit or reveal safety problems.
  • You could lose all of your money. The programme could stop at any stage.
  • You may not be able to sell. Permissioned tokens would have restricted transferability and there may be no market for them.
  • Long timelines. Drug development takes many years, and any return, if one ever occurs, is likely to be far in the future.
  • Regulatory and legal risk. Laws on digital assets and securities are changing and differ between countries.
  • Technology risk. Blockchain systems, smart contracts, wallets and custodians can fail, be hacked or be misused.
  • Not protected like a bank deposit. Investments of this kind are generally not covered by deposit or investor compensation schemes. [CONFIRM for each jurisdiction.]

Supporting research you care about is a good aim, but it is not a reason to invest money you cannot afford to lose. Anyone considering a future opportunity should seek independent financial and legal advice.
