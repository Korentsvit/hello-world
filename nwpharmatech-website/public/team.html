People and governance

The people behind the programme

Expertise in clinical high risk of psychosis, clinical trial design and cannabinoid science, alongside the company's management, board members and senior advisers.

Clinical and scientific leadership

Clinical high risk of psychosis (CHR-P)

Dr Scott W. Woods

CHR-P programme lead

Individual capacity

Psychiatrist and researcher who has worked for many years on identifying and helping young people at clinical high risk of psychosis.

Dr Woods contributes in an individual capacity. His Yale affiliation is given for identification only; Yale University does not endorse or sponsor the programme. Programme leadership is separate from the study sponsor role: NWPharmaTech is developing the proposed CHR-P programme, and formal study responsibilities will be documented before the study begins.

Responsibilities

  • Leads the CHR-P programme.

Affiliations

  • Professor of Psychiatry, Yale School of Medicine
  • Director, PRIME Clinic, which specialises in people at clinical high risk of psychosis

Clinical trial design

Dr John M. Kane

Lead, Schizophrenia Programme

Psychiatrist and schizophrenia researcher with long experience of leading clinical trials.

The schizophrenia programme is separate from the CHR-P programme.

Affiliations

  • Professor of Psychiatry and Molecular Medicine, Donald and Barbara Zucker School of Medicine at Hofstra/Northwell
  • Co-director, Institute for Behavioral Science, Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research

Cannabinoid neuropharmacology

Dr Grace Blest-Hopley

Partner & Chief Scientific Officer

Neuroscientist specialising in cannabinoid neuropharmacology. PhD from King's College London on the actions and effects of cannabinoids.

Leads the company's scientific work. She is not the CHR-P programme lead.

How clinical decisions stay independent

  • Clinical decisions, including study design, eligibility, safety and conduct, are kept independent of funding and financing.
  • Each study needs approval from the medicines regulator and a research ethics committee before it can start.
