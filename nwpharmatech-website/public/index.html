Research programme · Clinical high risk of psychosis

Careful research for young people at clinical high risk of psychosis

We are developing NWPT-SM32300, an investigational oral cannabidiol medicine, and planning a Phase 2B study. Whether it helps is not yet known. Finding out is the purpose of the research.

Read the programme brief For young people and families

The programme in 90 seconds

  1. 1 · The clinical need

    Young people at risk, with no licensed medicine

    Some young people who seek help meet criteria for clinical high risk of psychosis. In pooled studies, about one in four developed psychosis within three years. Source: Salazar de Pablo et al. 2021

    Clinical need

  2. 2 · The investigational candidate

    NWPT-SM32300

    A 300 mg oral softgel capsule of highly purified cannabidiol, in a self-emulsifying (micellar) lipid formulation. It is investigational; not approved by any medicines regulator for any use.

    Science and formulation

  3. 3 · The planned study

    A Phase 2B study in clinical high risk

    Designed to evaluate dose response, symptoms, safety and tolerability in people at clinical high risk of psychosis.

    The study: progress and evidence

  4. 4 · Current status

    Phase 1 completed; Phase 2B planned

    The Phase 1 study in healthy volunteers was completed in December 2025. The Phase 2B study is not recruiting. Protocol being finalised.

    Milestones and sources

Programme brief

The programme on one page

NWPharmaTech presents our research programme for people at clinical high risk of psychosis, explaining the clinical need, the science and the people behind it.

The programme centres on NWPT-SM32300, an investigational oral cannabidiol formulation, and a planned Phase 2B study evaluating dose response, symptoms, safety and tolerability.

Alongside the clinical programme, we are developing a financing route through which eligible investors could take part through a dedicated programme vehicle, potentially using permissioned tokens. Clinical decisions remain independent of financing, and the treatment's effectiveness is unproven.
