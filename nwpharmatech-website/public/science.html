The research

Science and formulation

Three questions behind the programme, and how firm the answer to each one is.

  1. Why investigate CBD in this setting?
  2. Why investigate a micellar softgel?
  3. Why study a dose range?

Question 1

Why investigate CBD in this setting?

Established

Biological rationale

Cannabidiol (CBD) and THC both come from the cannabis plant but act differently. CBD does not produce a "high". Daily use of high-THC cannabis is associated with higher odds of psychotic disorder. Source: Di Forti et al. 2019

No medicine is licensed for people at clinical high risk of psychosis, and NICE advises against antipsychotics for prevention. Source: NICE CG178

Emerging evidence

Findings from other CBD preparations

In small experiments, CBD and THC had opposite effects on some measures of brain activity. Source: Bhattacharyya et al. 2010 In people at clinical high risk, a single CBD dose partly shifted brain activity towards that of healthy volunteers during a memory task, Source: Bhattacharyya et al. 2018 and a small three-week study reported lower symptom scores than placebo. Source: Bhattacharyya et al. 2024

In schizophrenia, results are mixed: two small trials were favourable Source: McGuire et al. 2018 Source: Leweke et al. 2012 and one found no benefit. Source: Boggs et al. 2018

Programme information

About NWPT-SM32300

No study of NWPT-SM32300 has yet been done in people at clinical high risk. None of the findings above used NWPT-SM32300.

Open question

Unanswered

Whether CBD reduces symptoms, improves functioning or lowers the risk of psychosis in this group. A larger UK trial that could have answered this did not go ahead. Source: CANTOP-RCT report

Question 2

Why investigate a micellar softgel?

Established

The delivery problem

CBD dissolves poorly in water. Taken by mouth on an empty stomach, only about 6% of a dose is estimated to reach the bloodstream, and much is broken down by the liver first. A high-fat meal increases absorption several-fold, which makes exposure depend on what someone has eaten. Source: Perucca and Bialer 2020 Source: Taylor et al. 2018

Programme information

The design of NWPT-SM32300

NWPT-SM32300 is a 300 mg oral softgel capsule. Its contents are a self-emulsifying (micellar) lipid formulation: when the capsule opens in the gut, the contents are designed to disperse into very small droplets, a way of carrying poorly water-soluble medicines. A fixed-strength capsule also avoids measuring doses from an oil.

Open question

Not yet shown

Whether this formulation gives more consistent absorption than other CBD products, and how food affects it, has not been published. The Phase 1 study measured absorption; its results are not yet published. No study has measured how much reaches the brain.

Question 3

Why study a dose range?

Established

General principle

Benefits and side effects of a medicine can both depend on dose. With CBD, rises in liver enzymes are dose-related. Source: Epidyolex SmPC A Phase 2B study compares doses so that any later study can use the dose with the best balance of benefit and tolerability, or stop if none is adequate.

Emerging evidence

Doses used with other CBD preparations

Studies in people at clinical high risk used 600 mg a day. Source: Bhattacharyya et al. 2024 Trials in schizophrenia used between 600 and 1,000 mg a day, with mixed results. Source: Boggs et al. 2018 Source: McGuire et al. 2018 Doses cannot be transferred directly between products that are absorbed differently.

Programme information

NWPT-SM32300

Each capsule contains 300 mg, so doses are whole numbers of capsules. The Phase 1 study gave single doses of 600 mg and 900 mg to healthy volunteers. The doses for the Phase 2B study have not been confirmed.

Open question

Unanswered

Which dose, if any, helps people at clinical high risk, and which is best tolerated with daily use.
