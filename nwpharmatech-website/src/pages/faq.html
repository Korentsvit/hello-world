Urgent help

NWPharmaTech cannot respond to emergencies or give individual medical advice. If someone is in immediate danger, call the emergency number for your country.

United Kingdom

  • Emergency: 999
  • England and Wales, urgent mental health help: 111, option 2
  • Scotland: NHS 24 on 111 (mental health option)
  • Northern Ireland: Lifeline 0808 808 8000
  • Samaritans (free, 24 hours): 116 123
  • Shout (text service): text SHOUT to 85258

Ireland

  • Emergency: 112 or 999
  • Samaritans (free, 24 hours): 116 123
  • Pieta (suicide and self-harm crisis support, free, 24 hours): 1800 247 247

United States

  • Emergency: 911
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988

Last checked 23 September 2026. Elsewhere, contact your local emergency services.

About the investigational medicine

Does NWPT-SM32300 work?

We do not know. NWPT-SM32300 is an investigational medicine and its effectiveness is unproven. The completed Phase 1 study looked at safety and how the body handles it, not at whether it works. The planned Phase 2B study is intended to give the first evidence in people at clinical high risk, looking at {{fact:phase2b.aims}}.

Can I get NWPT-SM32300?

No. It is available only within approved clinical research, and the Phase 2B study is not recruiting. We cannot supply it on request.

Should I take shop-bought CBD to lower my risk of psychosis?

Please talk to a doctor first. There is no evidence that CBD products sold in shops or online prevent psychosis or help people at clinical high risk. They are not licensed medicines, and CBD can interact with other medicines. {{cite:epidyolex-smpc}}

Isn't cannabis linked to psychosis?

Daily use of high-THC cannabis is associated with a higher risk of psychosis. {{cite:diforti-2019}} Cannabidiol (CBD) is a different compound that does not produce a "high". Whether CBD helps in psychosis is still being researched. See the science.

About taking part

Is the Phase 2B study recruiting?

Not yet. It needs regulatory and research ethics approval before it can start, and it will be registered publicly before anyone is enrolled.

Who might be able to take part?

The study is planned for {{fact:phase2b.population}}. Its full eligibility criteria will be listed in its public registry entry.

Would taking part affect my usual care?

Taking part in any study is voluntary, and anyone taking part can leave at any time without it affecting their usual care. How the Phase 2B study would fit alongside usual care will be set out in its participant information.

About the programme

How is the programme funded?

What funding supports, who controls spending and how progress is reported are explained under funding and financing. We are developing a financing route for eligible investors, but no investment is being accepted. Clinical decisions are kept independent of funding.

Will study results be published?

Results of the completed Phase 1 study have not been published yet. A results summary is planned for both trial registries and this site; see the Phase 1 study. The Phase 2B study will be registered publicly before anyone is enrolled.

Is this website medical advice?

No. It gives general information about a research programme and should not be used to make decisions about your own care. Always speak to a qualified health professional.
