Questions & answers

Questions and answers

Answers for people at clinical high risk, families, clinicians and anyone interested in the programme.

About the treatment

Does NWPT-SM32300 work?

We do not know. NWPT-SM32300 is investigational, and its effectiveness is unproven. The planned Phase 2B study is designed to provide evidence on dose response, symptoms, safety and tolerability.

Can I get NWPT-SM32300?

No. It is available only within approved clinical research, and the study is not yet recruiting. We cannot supply it on request or outside a study.

Should I take CBD from a shop to reduce my risk of psychosis?

We would advise against doing so without talking to a doctor. There is no evidence that CBD products sold in shops or online prevent psychosis or help people at clinical high risk. Consumer products vary in content and quality, some contain THC, and CBD can interact with other medicines.

If you are worried about your mental health, please speak to your GP or a mental health professional.

Isn't cannabis linked to psychosis?

Heavy use of high-THC cannabis is associated with a higher risk of psychosis. Cannabidiol (CBD) is a different compound that does not produce a "high". Whether CBD has any benefit in psychosis is still being researched. See the science.

About taking part

Is the study recruiting?

Not yet. The study is being planned and needs regulatory and ethics approvals before it can start. Recruitment details will be published on the updates page and on the public trial registry.

Who might be able to take part?

The study is planned for people at clinical high risk of psychosis, as assessed by specialist services. Full eligibility criteria will be published when they are final. [PLACEHOLDER]

Would taking part affect my usual care?

Participants would continue to receive standard care throughout, and could withdraw at any time without it affecting their care.

Is this website medical advice?

No. This website provides general information about a research programme. It is not medical advice and should not be used to make decisions about your own care. Always speak to a qualified health professional.

Need help now?

If you or someone you know is in crisis, call 999 or go to A&E. For urgent mental health help in the UK, call NHS 111 and choose option 2, or call Samaritans free on 116 123. Outside the UK, contact your local emergency services.
