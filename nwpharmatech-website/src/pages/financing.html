About

Funding and financing

How the programme is paid for, who decides how money is spent, and how we report progress. Nothing on this page is an offer, and no investment is being accepted.

What funding supports

  • Running clinical studies, including the planned Phase 2B study
  • Manufacturing clinical supplies of {{fact:product.name}} to GMP standard
  • Regulatory, safety and quality work
  • Programme operations, data management and analysis

What is and is not funded

{{block:milestones:funding}}

Figures are published only once they are approved and can be shown as one of: planning target, committed funding, cash received, or money spent. Expressions of interest are never counted as funding.

Who controls spending and clinical decisions

Spending is controlled by the board of {{fact:company.name}}, the programme's company.

Clinical decisions (study design, eligibility, dosing, safety, analysis and publication) are made by the clinical and scientific leadership and approved by regulators and research ethics committees. Funders and investors have no say in them and no access to participant data.

How progress is reported

Dated events appear on the updates page, and study milestones with their sources on the study page. Programme reports, answering what changed, what supports it, what remains unresolved and what happens next, are planned; see the newsroom.

Reports are written for the public and are not investment communications.

The financing route in development

Programme information

Preferred: platform-integrated programme financing. Eligible investors would take part in a dedicated programme vehicle through a regulated investor platform, which would carry out eligibility, identity and anti-money-laundering checks. Where feasible, investors' interests would be issued as permissioned tokens at closing.

Fallback: register-first. Interests would be recorded on a conventional register of holders, with any digital representation added only if and when lawful and approved.

Eligibility would be set by the law of each country, is likely to be limited to professional, qualified or accredited investors, and would exclude some countries.
