What "Phase 2B" means

Medicines are tested in stages. Phase 1 studies look mainly at safety and how the body handles a medicine, usually in healthy volunteers. Phase 2 studies look at whether it may work in people with the condition, and at what dose.

A Phase 2B study typically compares more than one dose with a control, to understand the dose–response relationship and decide whether a larger Phase 3 study is justified.

Programme governance

A clinical study is governed by separate parties, each with its own duties under clinical trial law. They do not form one chain of command.

  • The sponsor is responsible for the study's design, dose and endpoints, safety reporting, the quality of the study medicine and the regulatory submissions. NWPharmaTech is developing the proposed CHR-P programme. Formal study responsibilities will be documented before the study begins.
  • Investigators run the study at each site, obtain participants' informed consent and are responsible for their care.
  • A research ethics committee reviews the study independently to protect participants, and must give a favourable opinion before it starts.
  • The medicines regulator in each country (in the UK, the MHRA) must authorise the study, and can inspect it, require changes or stop it.

Funders, financing participants and the public community have no part in these decisions. How the proposed financing would sit alongside them is explained under Who does what.

Protecting participants

The Phase 2B study can start only after approval by a research ethics committee and the relevant medicines regulators. Anyone taking part would give informed consent and could leave at any time. Cannabidiol can raise liver enzymes and interact with other medicines, {{cite:epidyolex-smpc}} so safety monitoring will be set out in the study protocol.

Can I take part?

Not yet: the Phase 2B study is not recruiting, and we cannot supply {{fact:product.name}} outside an approved study.
